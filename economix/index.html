Economix

Economix - Crowdsourced Rating
& Forecasting Ecosystem

Designing a full crypto-and-beyond investment ecosystem from the ground up - a public discovery site, tiered identity verification, and a platform where every rating and forecast is backed by real reputation.

My Role

UX Architect · Design Manager

Industry

Fintech · Crypto, Stocks & ICOs

Scope

74+ Screens · Web + Responsive · 0-to-1 IA

01 Background

What is Economix?

Economix is a decentralized platform that rates and forecasts crypto assets, stocks, ICOs, IPOs and startups through the collective judgment of its own users - professional raters, forecasters, and portfolio builders - instead of a handful of paid analysts. The core belief: no single expert's opinion is fully objective, but the average of many independent, incentivized opinions gets remarkably close.

My Role

I led the entire project from vision to product - product design and UX architecture end to end, as an independent designer over a six-month engagement, specifying the platform's information architecture, designing every screen across four surfaces (the public rating site, the trading site, onboarding, and the trading platform itself), and leading and managing a team of two designers. I also partnered closely on product strategy and roadmap decisions.

02 Problem Statements

Two compounding problems

Problem statement 01

No Reliable Signal

Evaluating an ICO or crypto asset meant trusting a handful of paid analysts or scattered social sentiment - there was no transparent, collective, accountable rating standard investors could actually check.

03 Research

Competitive Benchmarking

Feeling the Industry

I went through the live apps of the closest existing players - Estimize, Augur, CIndicator - not their marketing pages, to see how they actually handled forecasting, rating and portfolio UX before designing our own.

Finding 01

Reputation Next to Data

Followers and track record shown right next to the estimate - not buried in a separate profile.

Finding 02

Sliders Are Too Blunt

Fast to grasp, but too binary for a multi-criteria project rating.

Finding 03

Portfolio-First Home

Allocation, live assets, performance - makes a forecasting tool feel like somewhere you'd actually return to.

Motivation Research

Why People Actually Take Action

Knowing how rating apps work wasn't enough - I needed to know what actually gets someone to rate, forecast, or invest in the first place. Behavioral finance research on crypto markets points to one answer: it's rarely analysis. It's the live price board itself.

"...it is mainly individual investors who make decisions under the influence of emotions, not guided by rational analysis."

Klepacki, J. (2025) - European Research Studies Journal

"Cryptocurrencies' prices are driven by herding... prices 'as-is' are the coordination mechanism."

Poyser Calderón, O. (2018) - Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona

"84% of investors make their investment decisions based on the fear of missing out (FOMO)." (n=1,248 crypto holders)

Kraken Crypto FOMO Survey, 2024 - via The Defiant

What this proves: people don't act because they analyzed - they act because they saw the price move, and saw everyone else moving with it. The live number is the trigger, not a rational read of the fundamentals.

My clearest real-world reference for this was CoinMarketCap itself - a live, public ranking table is the trigger, not a landing page pitch.

This is why the discovery homepage leads with a live, public ranking table instead of a marketing pitch - the price movement itself is what gets someone to act, so the product has to earn that moment before it ever asks for a signup.

04 Execution

Step 01

Structuring the Data Model

With three different user actions feeding one reward economy, the real design problem wasn't the screens - it was the data model underneath them: what an action targets, what it produces, and how that loops back into who gets followed. I mapped this out before designing a single screen, and it drove every decision that came after it.

Step 02

Discovery - A Public, CoinMarketCap-Style Homepage

Before signing up, anyone can browse live project rankings, prices and scores. The homepage doubles as both marketing and product - it's the trust-building layer that gets people to create an account.

Step 03

Sign Up & Identity Verification Wizard

Creating an account is only step one - how much a user can actually invest depends on how far they go through identity verification (three tiers: up to 15K, up to 50K, up to 1M). I designed the full multi-step wizard, balancing regulatory requirements against drop-off.

Step 04

Rate & Forecast - Score Quality, Predict Price

Every Rate or Forecast starts here - a filterable list of every asset, by category, score, and criteria. This is the jumping-off point for both actions.

They aren't the same action - each one is its own flow, targeting a completely different thing.

Rating

Scores a project's underlying quality - team, tech, tokenomics - against fixed criteria, one at a time, until every criterion has a number behind it.

Forecasting

Predicts where the asset's real, tracked price will actually move by a set date - checked later against an outside oracle (CoinMarketCap), not against opinion.

Step 05

Asset Page - Not Just Crypto

Every rated asset - crypto, stocks, ICOs, IPOs, or startups - gets its own page with a live price chart, community rating, and a feed of what raters are actually saying.

Step 06

Dashboard - Your Whole Portfolio at a Glance

Once inside, the dashboard is home base - assets, open events, forecast accuracy, and contribution points, all in one modular view.

Step 07

Community - Following the Wisdom of the Crowd

Users follow top raters and forecasters, see leaderboards ranked by real accuracy and ROI, and grow their own reputation as their followers grow.
